The adventuring party — Chaerie Lunarblossom, an elf druid with a Raven's Feather relic; Plik Puddlefin, a dragonborn sorcerer unknowingly searching for his missing 'shell,' bearing a Dragon's Scale relic; and Maxine Lira Payne, a tiefling fighter with a Broken Blade relic — began their journey in the town of Daggerford, on the eve of Highharvesttide.

With the local inns full, the party took refuge in the stable of the Nightmare's Bridle, an old, worn-down tavern on the town's outskirts. As they settled in, their relics suddenly began to react to an unseen force, calling them toward a location known as Death House. The Broken Blade trembled and spun, the Dragon's Scale glowed and burned cold, and the Raven's Feather twirled away as though it had a will of its own. Drawn by these strange signs, the party followed the relics into the dense, swirling mist that swallowed the streets of Daggerford.

The festive laughter and warmth of Highharvesttide slowly faded as the mists thickened, leaving the town of Daggerford behind. The relics pulled the party deeper, their senses disoriented by the fog and echoes of a heartbeat that seemed to come from nowhere and everywhere. After a disorienting journey through the labyrinthine mists, the fog parted, revealing the ominous silhouette of a grand manor — Death House.

The house loomed before them, four stories tall with narrow windows and an air of malevolent grandeur. The wrought-iron gate creaked in the wind, oil lamps flickered dimly, and the oaken doors stood waiting as an uninviting entrance. With trepidation, the adventurers pushed through the gate and entered the manor.

Shortly after stepping inside, a grandfather clock struck six, and lines of eerie verse appeared in blood on the walls:

_Beneath this dwelling lurks a beast_  
_Who hungers for a bloody feast._  
_He sleeps until the midnight chime_  
_Then wakes to feed his dark design._  
_If morsels seek to flee their doom,_  
_Then bring toward his secret room_  
_A gift to soothe his savage mood_  
_But mind the servants of his brood._

The party began to search the house. They encountered a dining room filled with the sound of laughter from outside, but the moment they entered, silence reigned. The haunting sense of the house's secrets grew as they uncovered signs of infidelity and domestic strife.

On the second floor, they found clues suggesting that Klara, a resident of the house, had become pregnant with Mr. Durst's child. The party also discovered a hidden room in the conservatory after playing the "Waltz for Klara" on the harpsichord. Inside the secret room, they found a chilling letter addressed to Mrs. Durst, written by Strahd von Zarovich. 

Towards the end of the session, the party climbed to the third floor of the house, still searching for answers that might lead to their escape. The atmosphere grew increasingly oppressive, and they found themselves face-to-face with dangers lurking in the shadows. The tension reached its peak as they rolled for initiative, ending the session on a cliffhanger.
